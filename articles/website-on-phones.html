Your website on phones: the 5 most common breaks and how to check your own

A site can look fine on a computer and still be hard to use on a phone. Here is what we look for, and how you can check your own.

Plenty of people see a website on a phone first, sometimes while standing in a parking lot. A page that looks fine on a computer can still be hard to read or use on a small screen. Here is how to check your own site, and five common breaks we check for.

How to check

  • On your own phone. Open your site in the phone's web browser, not a preview inside your site builder. Look at each main page held upright and then turned sideways.
  • On a second phone. If you can, borrow one that is different from yours, like an iPhone if you have Android, or an older, smaller phone.
  • On a computer. Chrome and Edge have a device view in their developer tools. Press F12 (Cmd+Option+I on a Mac), or right-click the page and choose Inspect, then press Ctrl+Shift+M (Cmd+Shift+M on a Mac) to switch to a phone-sized view. Firefox has Responsive Design Mode with Ctrl+Shift+M (Cmd+Option+M on a Mac). These views are close to a real phone but not exact, so use them alongside your own phone.

1. The page slides sideways

On a phone, a page should only scroll up and down. If you can swipe it left and right, something is wider than the screen: often a large image, a table, or an embedded map or video with a fixed width. Find the section where the edge sticks out. Most builders have a setting to make images and embeds fit the width of the screen.

2. The text is tiny and needs pinching

If you have to pinch and zoom to read a paragraph, the phone may be showing a shrunken copy of the desktop page. Modern themes usually handle this. On a hand-built or older site, the page's head section needs a viewport tag, which looks like this: <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1">

3. The menu will not open, or will not close

Phone menus usually hide behind a button with three lines. Tap it, tap every item in it, and make sure you can close it again. Check that your most important pages, like contact, hours, menu or services, can be reached from it.

4. The phone number and address are not tappable

Tap your phone number. Your phone should offer to call it. Tap your email address and it should open an email app. If nothing happens, the number is plain text instead of a link. A phone link starts with tel: followed by the number, like tel:+15555550123, and an email link starts with mailto:. Many builders have a phone or email option when you add a link or button. You can also link your address to a map so it opens directions.

5. Something covers the page, or it loads slowly

Pop-ups, chat bubbles and sign-up boxes can cover most of a small screen, and their close buttons can be tiny. Make sure each one can be closed easily with a thumb. Large photos can also make a page slow on mobile data. Load your home page on mobile data instead of Wi-Fi and notice how long it takes before you can read and tap. Resizing photos to about the size they actually show on the page before you upload them often helps. Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool at pagespeed.web.dev gives a phone report with suggestions.

Keep notes as you go

Write down each problem, the page it is on and the phone you used. Fix one thing at a time and check again on the phone afterward. It is also worth testing your contact form from a phone. Our 10-minute self-check for broken images, links and contact forms walks through that step by step.
