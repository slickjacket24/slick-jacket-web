Broken images, links and contact forms: a 10-minute self-check

Three kinds of breaks can quietly turn visitors away. Here is a quick way to check for all three yourself.

Broken images, dead links and contact forms that never deliver are easy to miss, because the site owner rarely uses the site the way a visitor does. This check takes about ten minutes for a small site. All you need is a computer, a phone, something to take notes on, and an email address you can check that is different from the one your form sends to.

Minutes 1 to 3: images

  • Scroll through every main page on your computer and then on your phone.
  • A broken image usually shows up as an empty box, a small broken-picture icon, or a blank gap with a few words in it.
  • Common causes: the image was deleted or renamed in your media library, the site moved to a new address and the image still points to the old one, or the image is loaded from somewhere else that no longer has it, like an old file-sharing link.
  • To fix it, upload the image again to your own site's media library and put it back on the page.
  • While you are there, check that each image has a short written description in its settings, often called alt text. It helps people who use screen readers, and it shows in place of the image if the image ever fails to load.

Minutes 4 to 6: links

  • Click every item in your menu, on the computer and on the phone.
  • Click the links in your footer and your social media icons. Make sure they open your current profiles, not an old or empty account.
  • Click your buttons, like Book, Order or Directions, and make sure each one does what it says.
  • Write down any link that lands on a “page not found” or 404 page, or on the wrong page.
  • To fix one, edit the link in your site builder so it points to the right page. If you renamed a page, many builders and plugins let you set up a redirect from the old address to the new one, so old links keep working.
  • The free W3C Link Checker at validator.w3.org/checklink can check the links on one page for you. Click anything it flags yourself before changing it, since some working sites block automatic checkers.

Minutes 7 to 10: contact forms

  • Fill out your own form the way a customer would. Use your other email address and write “Test” and today's date in the message.
  • After you press send, look for a clear thank-you or confirmation message. If nothing happens or an error appears, that is a break.
  • Check the inbox that should receive the message, and its spam or junk folder too.
  • If the message never arrives, check the form's settings for the address it sends to. It may still be an old email address. On some setups, including many self-hosted WordPress sites, form emails get filtered or rejected. A plugin that sends the site's email through a real email account (often called an SMTP plugin) is a common fix.
  • When the test arrives, press Reply. The reply should go to the address you typed in the form, not back to your own website.
  • Send one more test from your phone.

After the ten minutes

You now have a short list. Fix the contact form first if it failed, since that is how people reach you. Then fix links, then images. Run the same check every month or two, and again any time you change your site, your email or your web host.
