5 small breaks we keep seeing on local business websites

We look at a lot of local business websites. The same small breaks show up again and again. They don't usually mean the site needs a rebuild. They're the kind of thing a visitor notices, and a short, careful fix can clear up.

1. A link that doesn't go anywhere

A button, a menu item, or a footer link opens a page that isn't there. The visitor sees a "we couldn't find this page" message, or a blank dead end, instead of the thing they came for.

Why it matters: they were trying to view a menu, request a quote, or read about a service, and the path they picked just ends.

The fix is usually small: point the link at the live page, or take the broken one out of the menu so it isn't sitting there.

2. A copyright year that's years behind

The site may be open and current, but the footer still shows an old year, sometimes several years behind. That one line can make the whole page feel older than it is.

Why it matters: a stale year can make a current business look like it hasn't been tended to in a long time.

The fix is usually small: update the footer year so it matches the current year.

3. Hours that aren't on the site

Some sites list a phone and an address, but none of the pages say when the business is open. In some cases we checked, the hours were already on the Google profile, just not on the website.

Why it matters: a visitor who is already on your site still has to leave, or call, just to find out if you're open.

The fix is usually small: add the hours you want customers to see, next to the address or in the footer, on the pages people actually check.

4. A leftover "under construction" or placeholder page

Some sites still open a coming-soon page, or have leftover sample text in a template block.

Why it matters: a first-time visitor may think you aren't open yet, or that the page wasn't finished.

The fix is usually small: make the live site the page people land on first, and replace or remove leftover placeholder copy.

5. No phone number to tap

Some sites never show a shop phone, and the pages have no tappable call link. A visitor on a phone has no easy way to call from the site itself.

Why it matters: someone who is ready to call has to hunt for a number somewhere else, or they give up.

The fix is usually small: add the number you want customers to use, and make it tappable on a phone.
