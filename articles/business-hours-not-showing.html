Why your business hours don't show (on your site or Google) and how to fix it

Most hours problems come from a short list of causes. Here is how we check each one, so you can fix them yourself.

When someone looks up a business, the hours are one of the first things they check. If the hours are wrong, people may skip the trip or show up to a locked door.

Your hours usually live in two separate places: your own website and your Google Business Profile. Changing one does not automatically change the other, so we check them one at a time.

Part 1: Hours on your own website

The hours are inside a picture

Some sites show hours only inside an image, like a photo of the door sign. Search engines and screen readers may not read it, and it is easy to forget to swap the picture when hours change. Type your hours as plain text on the page.

The hours are only in a spot phones hide

Some site builders and themes hide sections on small screens. If your hours only live there, phone visitors never see them. Open your site on your own phone and scroll the whole home page and contact page. If the hours are not there, add them to a section that shows on every screen size.

Different hours in different places

It is easy to update the home page and forget the contact page, footer or a PDF menu. Write down every place your hours appear, including your social media profiles, and update all of them together.

You changed them, but the old hours still show

You may be seeing a saved copy. Check the page in a private or incognito window. If your builder has a publish step, make sure the change was published and not only saved as a draft. If your site uses a caching plugin, clear its cache.

Part 2: Hours on Google

The hours Google shows in Search and Maps come from your Google Business Profile. Google builds it from what the owner enters and from other sources, including your website, so the profile is the direct place to fix them.

Make sure you can manage the profile

To edit a profile, you need to be signed in to the Google account linked to it. Find your profile on Google Search or in the Google Maps app and look for Edit profile. If you can't find it, the profile may be managed by a different account. Google has a Request Access step for a profile someone else owns. The current owner gets an email and has 3 days to respond. Some businesses also need to verify their profile before they can make changes.

Set regular hours, then special hours

In the profile editor, go to Hours and set your regular weekly hours first. Then use special hours for holidays, special events and other short changes, so your normal week stays the same. You can also set More hours for services with their own times, like delivery, takeout or pickup, once your regular hours are set. For a longer closure, Google says to mark the business as temporarily closed instead of using special hours.

Give it time, then look again

Google reviews profile edits before they show. Google says this usually takes up to 10 minutes but can sometimes take up to 30 days. Then check your listing on your phone and in Google Maps.

Check back now and then

Google gathers business info from sources like user reports, and it may update your profile if those sources say something is out of date. For some suggested edits, Google may notify you first by email or in your profile, and you have 4 days to accept or reject them. Look at your listing every month and after every holiday.

A five-minute routine

  1. Read your hours out loud from your website, on your phone.
  2. Read them out loud from your Google listing.
  3. Compare both with your door sign.
  4. Fix whichever one is wrong, everywhere it appears.
  5. Put a reminder on your calendar to set special hours before the next holiday.
